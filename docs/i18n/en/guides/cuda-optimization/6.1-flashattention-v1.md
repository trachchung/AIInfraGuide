## 1. Performance bottlenecks of standard Attention

### 1.1 Standard Attention compute flow

Scaled Dot-Product Attention runs as:

$$
S = QK^\top / \sqrt{d}
$$

$$
P = \text{softmax}(S)
$$

$$
O = PV
$$

where $Q, K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$, $N$ is sequence length, and $d$ is head dimension.

### 1.2 Memory bottleneck analysis

The key problem in the standard implementation is that intermediate matrices $S$ and $P$ are $N \times N$:

| 📊 Metric | 📝 Value | 💡 Note |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Intermediate matrix size | $O(N^2)$ | S and P each need $N \times N$ storage |
| HBM read/write | $O(N^2)$ | Repeatedly read/write $N \times N$ matrices |
| Compute | $O(N^2 d)$ | Two matrix multiplies |

At sequence length $N = 4096$, $d = 128$, a single head's $S$ alone needs $4096 \times 4096 \times 2 = 32$ MB (FP16). With multiple heads and batches, memory cost is huge.

### 1.3 Insufficient arithmetic intensity

Arithmetic Intensity of standard Attention is:

$$
\text{AI} = \frac{O(N^2 d)}{O(N^2 + Nd)} \approx O(d)
$$

Since $d$ is usually only 64 or 128, arithmetic intensity is low — the bottleneck is memory bandwidth, not compute. A lot of time goes to data movement while GPU compute units starve.

💡 **Tip**: like a chef who cooks fast but waits forever for ingredients from the warehouse — the core bottleneck of standard Attention is data movement between HBM and SRAM.

## 2. Core idea of FlashAttention

<img src="/AIInfraGuide/images/flashattentionv1-0.png" alt="" style="max-width: 100%; display: block; margin: 0 auto;" />

### 2.1 IO-Awareness: focus on data movement

FlashAttention's core insight: **on modern GPUs, Attention is Memory-Bound; the win is fewer HBM accesses, not fewer FLOPs.**

Traditional optimization chases fewer FLOPs (e.g. sparse Attention). FlashAttention takes another path — clever tiling keeps data in SRAM (Shared Memory) as much as possible and avoids landing intermediate results in HBM.

### 2.2 Two key techniques

FlashAttention V1 combines two techniques:

1. **Tiling**: cut Q, K, V into small tiles; load one tile at a time into SRAM to compute
2. **Online Softmax**: without knowing the global max, correct Softmax results as new tiles arrive

📌 **Key point**: both are required — Tiling solves "how not to materialize the full $N \times N$ matrix"; Online Softmax solves "Softmax needs global info when computing in tiles".

### 2.3 Intuition

Imagine ranking every student by percentile from the whole class's scores. The usual way collects every score (all on the blackboard) then computes. FlashAttention is more like: each time a group of scores arrives, immediately update running stats (max, sum) and correct previous results — the final answer matches collecting everything first.

## 3. Online Softmax algorithm

### 3.1 Three passes of standard Softmax

For numerical stability, standard (safe) Softmax needs three passes over $x \in \mathbb{R}^N$:

**Pass 1**: global max for numerical stability

$$
m = \max_{i=1}^{N} x_{i}
$$

**Pass 2**: normalization denominator (exp-sum)

$$
\ell = \sum_{j=1}^{N} e^{x_j - m}
$$

**Pass 3**: normalized output per element

$$
\text{softmax}(x_i) = \frac{e^{x_i - m}}{\ell}
$$

Passes 2 and 3 both need the global max $m$ from pass 1; pass 3 also needs $\ell$ from pass 2. You must finish a full scan before the next — which conflicts with tiling.

> 📖 **Further reading**: Online Softmax (NVIDIA, 2018) merges the three passes into two — maintain $m$ and $\ell$ while scanning; FlashAttention further fuses "multiply by $V$ for the output" into that flow.

### 3.2 Online Softmax recurrence

The key innovation: maintain **correctable** local stats and update them as new data tiles arrive.

Suppose the first $j-1$ tiles are done, with local max $m^{(j-1)}$ and local exp-sum $l^{(j-1)}$. When tile $j$, $x^{(j)}$, arrives:

**Step 1**: update max

$$
m^{(j)} = \max(m^{(j-1)}, \max(x^{(j)}))
$$

**Step 2**: correct and update exp-sum

$$
l^{(j)} = l^{(j-1)} \cdot e^{m^{(j-1)} - m^{(j)}} + \sum_{i} e^{x^{(j)}_i - m^{(j)}}
$$

**Step 3**: correct existing output

$$
O^{(j)} = O^{(j-1)} \cdot \frac{l^{(j-1)} \cdot e^{m^{(j-1)} - m^{(j)}}}{l^{(j)}} + \frac{\sum_i e^{x^{(j)}_i - m^{(j)}} \cdot V^{(j)}_i}{l^{(j)}}
$$

### 3.3 Correctness

Mathematical correctness comes from a simple identity:

$$
e^{x_i - m_{\text{new}}} = e^{x_i - m_{\text{old}}} \cdot e^{m_{\text{old}} - m_{\text{new}}}
$$

Whenever the global max updates, every previous exp term is fixed by multiplying $e^{m_{\text{old}} - m_{\text{new}}}$. The operation is exact — **no approximation**.

⚠️ **Note**: Online Softmax is mathematically equivalent to standard Softmax (aside from tiny rounding differences from floating-point order). It is not an approximate algorithm.

## 4. Tiling strategy

### 4.1 Tiling scheme

Split $Q$, $K$, $V$ along the sequence dimension into tiles of size $B_r$ (Q tile size) and $B_c$ (K/V tile size):

- $Q$ into $T_r = \lceil N / B_r \rceil$ tiles
- $K, V$ into $T_c = \lceil N / B_c \rceil$ tiles

<img src="/AIInfraGuide/images/flashattentionv1-1.png" alt="Tiling strategy" style="max-width: 85%; display: block; margin: 0 auto;" />

Tile size depends on SRAM capacity $M$ (in elements; think `M = SRAM_bytes / sizeof(dtype)`) and head dimension $d$ (feature dim per token, e.g. $d = 128$ in LLaMA-7B):

$$
B_c = \left\lceil \frac{M}{4d} \right\rceil, \quad B_r = \min\left(\left\lceil \frac{M}{4d} \right\rceil, d\right)
$$

**Why $\dfrac{M}{4d}$?**

Computing one tile, SRAM must hold $Q_i, K_j, V_j, O_i$ — four tensors roughly $B \times d$ (see §4.2), about $4 B d$ elements. Require $4 B d \le M$, so each tile holds at most $\dfrac{M}{4d}$ rows. Relatively small $S_{ij}$ ($B_r \times B_c$) and stats $m_i, l_i$ ($2B_r$) are ignored because when $B \ll d$ they do not dominate SRAM.

**Why also $\min$ $B_r$ with $d$?**

Online Softmax's correction $e^{m^{(j-1)} - m^{(j)}}$ is a **per-row** scalar. The original paper constrains $B_r \le d$ to simplify analysis and avoid complicated stats management when rows greatly exceed the column dim; V2 relaxes this.

### 4.2 SRAM layout

During compute, SRAM must hold:

| 📊 Data | Size | 📝 Use |
|---------|------|---------|
| $Q_i$ tile | $B_r \times d$ | Current Query tile |
| $K_j$ tile | $B_c \times d$ | Current Key tile |
| $V_j$ tile | $B_c \times d$ | Current Value tile |
| $S_{ij}$ tile | $B_r \times B_c$ | Local attention scores |
| $O_i$ tile | $B_r \times d$ | Accumulated output tile |
| $m_i, l_i$ | $2 B_r$ | Row max, row exp-sum (one scalar per row) |

Total SRAM is about $(2B_r + 2B_c) \times d + B_r \times B_c + 2B_r$; the dominant term is $(2B_r + 2B_c) \times d$, which is why the tile-size formula has $4d$ in the denominator — stay under Shared Memory capacity $M$.

### 4.3 Outer and inner loops

FlashAttention V1 uses **outer loop over K/V tiles, inner loop over Q tiles**:

```
outer: for j = 1 to T_c (iterate K/V tiles)
    load K_j, V_j from HBM into SRAM
    inner: for i = 1 to T_r (iterate Q tiles)
        load Q_i, O_i, m_i, l_i from HBM into SRAM
        compute local scores S_ij = Q_i * K_j^T
        update stats and output
        write updated O_i, m_i, l_i back to HBM
```

💡 **Tip**: outer over K/V means each K/V tile loads from HBM once, while Q and O load many times. That is a V1 weakness (V2 flips to outer over Q).

## 5. Full forward pass

### 5.1 Step-by-step derivation

For the $i$-th Q tile and $j$-th K/V tile:

**Step 1**: local attention scores

$$
S_{ij} = Q_i K_j^\top / \sqrt{d} \in \mathbb{R}^{B_r \times B_c}
$$

**Step 2**: current tile row-max

$$
\tilde{m}_{ij} = \text{rowmax}(S_{ij}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B_r}
$$

**Step 3**: update global row-max

$$
m_i^{\text{new}} = \max(m_i, \tilde{m}_{ij})
$$

**Step 4**: correction factor and update exp-sum

$$
\alpha = e^{m_i - m_i^{\text{new}}}
$$

$$
l_i^{\text{new}} = \alpha \cdot l_i + \text{rowsum}\left(e^{S_{ij} - m_i^{\text{new}}}\right)
$$

$\alpha$ rescales the old exp-sum $l_i$ (under old max $m_i$) onto the new max $m_i^{\text{new}}$.

**Step 5**: update output

$$
O_i^{\text{new}} = \frac{\alpha \cdot l_i}{l_i^{\text{new}}} \cdot O_i + \frac{1}{l_i^{\text{new}}} \cdot e^{S_{ij} - m_i^{\text{new}}} \cdot V_j
$$


### 5.2 Algorithm pseudocode

```python
# FlashAttention V1 forward pseudocode
def flash_attention_forward(Q, K, V, B_r, B_c):
    N, d = Q.shape
    O = zeros(N, d)
    m = full(N, -inf)  # row max
    l = zeros(N)        # row exp-sum

    # Outer loop: iterate K/V tiles
    for j in range(0, N, B_c):
        K_j = K[j:j+B_c]  # load from HBM
        V_j = V[j:j+B_c]  # load from HBM

        # Inner loop: iterate Q tiles
        for i in range(0, N, B_r):
            Q_i = Q[i:i+B_r]          # load from HBM
            O_i = O[i:i+B_r]          # load from HBM
            m_i = m[i:i+B_r]          # load from HBM
            l_i = l[i:i+B_r]          # load from HBM

            # Local attention scores
            S_ij = Q_i @ K_j.T / sqrt(d)  # [B_r, B_c]

            # Update stats
            m_new = max(m_i, rowmax(S_ij))
            l_new = l_i * exp(m_i - m_new) + rowsum(exp(S_ij - m_new))

            # Update output
            O_new = O_i * (l_i * exp(m_i - m_new) / l_new).unsqueeze(1)
            O_new += (exp(S_ij - m_new) / l_new.unsqueeze(1)) @ V_j

            # Write back to HBM
            O[i:i+B_r] = O_new
            m[i:i+B_r] = m_new
            l[i:i+B_r] = l_new

    return O
```

### 5.3 Numerical stability

The whole path stays stable by subtracting the max:

- Every exp input is shifted by the current known max, so $e^x$ has $x \leq 0$
- Correction $e^{m_{\text{old}} - m_{\text{new}}}$ also has $m_{\text{old}} \leq m_{\text{new}}$, hence $\leq 1$

No overflow in the full computation.

## 6. Backward pass and recomputation

### 6.1 Backward challenge

Standard Attention backward needs intermediate $S$ and $P$ ($N \times N$) for gradients. $S = QK^\top / \sqrt{d}$ is pre-softmax logits; $P = \text{softmax}(S)$ is post-softmax attention weights; output $O = PV$:

$$
dV = P^\top dO
$$

$$
dP = dO \cdot V^\top
$$

$$
dS = P \odot \left(dP - \text{rowsum}(dP \odot P) \cdot \mathbf{1}^\top\right)
$$

FlashAttention uses the identity $\text{rowsum}(dP \odot P) = \text{rowsum}(dO \odot O)$ (written $D_i$) to avoid building the full $dP$ matrix.

Saving those intermediates would waste the memory the forward pass saved.

### 6.2 Recomputation strategy

FlashAttention V1 **does not save $S$ and $P$; it recomputes them in backward**:

- Forward only saves: $Q, K, V, O, m, l$ (inputs, output, stats)
- Backward: with saved $m$ and $l$, reload $Q_i, K_j$ to form $S_{ij}$, then recover $P_{ij}$

$$
P_{ij} = \text{diag}(l_i)^{-1} \cdot e^{S_{ij} - m_i}
$$

### 6.3 Cost of recomputation

Recomputation adds FLOPs (about one extra forward matmul), but HBM traffic drops:

| ✅ Gain | ❌ Cost |
|---------|---------|
| Memory $O(N^2)$ → $O(N)$ | Extra FLOPs (~50% of forward compute) |
| Much less HBM traffic | SRAM must hold more data at once |
| Longer sequences trainable | Backward kernel more complex |

Because Attention is Memory-Bound, the extra compute is "hidden" by fewer HBM accesses — wall-clock time is often shorter.

## 7. IO complexity analysis

### 7.1 Standard Attention IO complexity

$$
\text{HBM 访问量} = O(Nd + N^2) = O(N^2)
$$

$Nd$ comes from reading/writing $Q, K, V, O$ ($N \times d$); $N^2$ from intermediate $S$ and $P$ (each $N \times N$). When $N \gg d$, $N^2$ dominates → $O(N^2)$.

### 7.2 FlashAttention IO complexity

FlashAttention HBM traffic:

$$
\text{HBM 访问量} = O\left(\frac{N^2 d^2}{M}\right)
$$

Breakdown:

- From §4.1, $B_c = O(M/d)$, so $T_c = N / B_c = O(Nd/M)$ outer iterations
- Each outer pass sweeps full $Q$ and $O$ ($O(Nd)$ elements), so $Q$ and $O$ traffic is $T_c \cdot Nd = O(N^2 d^2 / M)$
- $K, V$ are each read once: $O(Nd)$, negligible next to the above

Total $O(N^2 d^2 / M)$. Intuition: **larger SRAM ($M$) → larger tiles → fewer outer loops → less repeated $Q/O$ traffic.**

$M$ is SRAM size in elements. Since $d^2 < M$ (typical $d = 128$, $M$ ≈ $192 \text{KB} / 2 = 98304$ FP16 elements, $d^2 = 16384$), this is much smaller than $O(N^2)$.

### 7.3 Lower-bound proof

The paper also shows any exact Attention algorithm needs at least $\Omega(N^2 d^2 / M)$ HBM accesses — FlashAttention's IO complexity is asymptotically optimal.

### 7.4 Practical speedup

Typical A100 comparison:

| 📊 Sequence length | Standard Attention | FlashAttention V1 | Speedup |
|------------|---------------|-------------------|--------|
| 1024 | 1.0x | 2.4x | 2.4x |
| 2048 | 1.0x | 2.8x | 2.8x |
| 4096 | 1.0x | 3.5x | 3.5x |
| 8192 | OOM | runnable | - |

## 8. Implementation notes and code examples

### 8.1 Tensor shapes end to end

Inputs $Q, K, V$ are 4-D; the first two dims are batch and head (passed through as "batch"); the last two do the matmuls:

```bash
Q: [B, H, N, D]
K: [B, H, N, D]   →  K^T (transpose last two dims): [B, H, D, N]
V: [B, H, N, D]

Step 1  Attention scores:
  S = Q @ K^T / sqrt(D)
    = [B, H, N, D] @ [B, H, D, N]
    = [B, H, N, N]

Step 2  Softmax (along last N, row-wise):
  P = softmax(S, dim=-1)
    shape: [B, H, N, N]

Step 3  Output:
  O = P @ V
    = [B, H, N, N] @ [B, H, N, D]
    = [B, H, N, D]   (same shape as Q)
```

Auxiliary tensors (FlashAttention-specific):

- `M: [B, H, N]` — per-row max (numerical stability + Online Softmax correction)
- `L: [B, H, N]` — per-row exp-sum (softmax denominator)

### 8.2 Kernel design notes

> 📌 **On parallel partitioning (grid and block design)**:
> - **grid (program granularity) = (B, H)**: each program owns full attention for one `(batch, head)` slice; programs are independent, no communication.
> - **Threads inside a block**: within a program, Triton splits `tl.dot`, `tl.max`, `tl.sum`, etc. across warps of a thread block. Tile size is set by `BLOCK_M` (Q rows $B_r$), `BLOCK_N` (K/V rows $B_c$), `BLOCK_D` (vectorized width on D).
> - **Serial inside a program**: outer loop over K/V tiles, inner over Q tiles
>
> Different Q tiles share stats $m_i, l_i$ and accumulated $O_i$ across outer iterations; one program's registers cannot hold m/l/O for "all Q tiles", so HBM auxiliaries `M, L` persist across iterations (the root cause of V1's extra IO — V2 swaps loop order, puts Q on the grid, and keeps m/l/O in registers the whole time, removing that HBM traffic).
>
> ⚠️ **V1's natural parallelism bottleneck**: grid size $B \times H$ in inference is often only tens (e.g. batch=1, heads=32 → 32 programs), while modern GPUs have 80–140 SMs (A100=108, H100=132), so SM utilization is ~30%. That is V1's main weakness and the reason V2 had to appear.


1. **Thread Block mapping**: in V1 each Thread Block owns one outer K/V tile (one $j$) and walks all Q tiles
2. **Shared Memory**: pre-allocate SRAM for $K_j$, $V_j$ (resident in the outer loop) plus $Q_i$, $S_{ij}$
3. **Registers**: keep stats $m_i$, $l_i$ and accumulated $O_i$ in registers when possible
4. **Loop structure**: outer loads K/V tiles; inner does GEMM and Softmax updates

### 8.3 Simplified Triton implementation

```python
import math
import torch
import triton
import triton.language as tl

@triton.jit
def flash_attn_v1_kernel(
    Q, K, V, O,    # [B, H, N, D]
    M, L,          # row max and row exp-sum
    stride_qb, stride_qh, stride_qn, stride_qd,
    stride_kb, stride_kh, stride_kn, stride_kd,
    stride_vb, stride_vh, stride_vn, stride_vd,
    stride_ob, stride_oh, stride_on, stride_od,
    stride_mb, stride_mh,
    N,
    D: tl.constexpr,        # head dimension (feature length per token); used in scale = 1/sqrt(D)
    BLOCK_M: tl.constexpr,  # Q tile rows B_r
    BLOCK_N: tl.constexpr,  # K/V tile rows B_c
    BLOCK_D: tl.constexpr,  # load/compute width on head dim; when D is a power of 2, BLOCK_D==D; else BLOCK_D > D and excess positions are masked
):
    # ========== V1 parallel partition: grid = (batch, head) ==========
    # Each program serially sweeps all K/V and Q tiles for one (batch, head)
    batch_id = tl.program_id(0)
    head_id = tl.program_id(1)

    # Base offsets of current (batch, head) on each tensor
    qkvo_bh_q = batch_id * stride_qb + head_id * stride_qh
    qkvo_bh_k = batch_id * stride_kb + head_id * stride_kh
    qkvo_bh_v = batch_id * stride_vb + head_id * stride_vh
    qkvo_bh_o = batch_id * stride_ob + head_id * stride_oh
    ml_bh = batch_id * stride_mb + head_id * stride_mh

    d_range = tl.arange(0, BLOCK_D)
    scale = 1.0 / math.sqrt(D)

    # ============ Outer loop: iterate K/V tiles (V1 key design) ============
    for kv_start in range(0, N, BLOCK_N):
        kv_range = kv_start + tl.arange(0, BLOCK_N)
        kv_mask = kv_range < N

        # Load K_j, V_j once; stay in registers/SRAM; reused by all Q tiles
        k = tl.load(K + qkvo_bh_k + kv_range[:, None] * stride_kn + d_range[None, :] * stride_kd,
                    mask=kv_mask[:, None] & (d_range[None, :] < D), other=0.0)
        v = tl.load(V + qkvo_bh_v + kv_range[:, None] * stride_vn + d_range[None, :] * stride_vd,
                    mask=kv_mask[:, None] & (d_range[None, :] < D), other=0.0)

        # ============ Inner loop: iterate Q tiles ============
        for q_start in range(0, N, BLOCK_M):
            q_range = q_start + tl.arange(0, BLOCK_M)
            q_mask = q_range < N

            # Load Q_i, O_i, m_i, l_i — in V1 every outer iter reloads from HBM
            q = tl.load(Q + qkvo_bh_q + q_range[:, None] * stride_qn + d_range[None, :] * stride_qd,
                        mask=q_mask[:, None] & (d_range[None, :] < D), other=0.0)
            o_i = tl.load(O + qkvo_bh_o + q_range[:, None] * stride_on + d_range[None, :] * stride_od,
                          mask=q_mask[:, None] & (d_range[None, :] < D), other=0.0).to(tl.float32)
            m_i = tl.load(M + ml_bh + q_range, mask=q_mask, other=float('-inf'))
            l_i = tl.load(L + ml_bh + q_range, mask=q_mask, other=0.0)

            # S_ij = Q_i @ K_j^T / sqrt(d)
            s = tl.dot(q, tl.trans(k)) * scale
            # Mask out-of-range K/V columns so fake 0s do not pollute the softmax denominator
            s = tl.where(kv_mask[None, :], s, float('-inf'))

            # Online Softmax update
            m_new = tl.maximum(m_i, tl.max(s, axis=1))
            alpha = tl.exp(m_i - m_new)
            p = tl.exp(s - m_new[:, None])
            l_new = alpha * l_i + tl.sum(p, axis=1)

            # Output correction + accumulate (matches §5.1 Step 5)
            o_new = o_i * (alpha * l_i / l_new)[:, None] \
                    + tl.dot(p.to(v.dtype), v) / l_new[:, None]

            # Write updated O_i, m_i, l_i back to HBM for the next outer iter
            tl.store(O + qkvo_bh_o + q_range[:, None] * stride_on + d_range[None, :] * stride_od,
                     o_new.to(O.dtype.element_ty),
                     mask=q_mask[:, None] & (d_range[None, :] < D))
            tl.store(M + ml_bh + q_range, m_new, mask=q_mask)
            tl.store(L + ml_bh + q_range, l_new, mask=q_mask)


def flash_attn_v1(Q, K, V, BLOCK_M=64, BLOCK_N=64):
    # Q, K, V: [B, H, N, D]
    B, H, N, D = Q.shape
    O = torch.zeros_like(Q)
    M = torch.full((B, H, N), float('-inf'), device=Q.device, dtype=torch.float32)
    L = torch.zeros((B, H, N), device=Q.device, dtype=torch.float32)

    BLOCK_D = triton.next_power_of_2(D)
    grid = (B, H)  # V1 parallel: only on batch×head
    flash_attn_v1_kernel[grid](
        Q, K, V, O, M, L,
        Q.stride(0), Q.stride(1), Q.stride(2), Q.stride(3),
        K.stride(0), K.stride(1), K.stride(2), K.stride(3),
        V.stride(0), V.stride(1), V.stride(2), V.stride(3),
        O.stride(0), O.stride(1), O.stride(2), O.stride(3),
        M.stride(0), M.stride(1),
        N, D=D, BLOCK_M=BLOCK_M, BLOCK_N=BLOCK_N, BLOCK_D=BLOCK_D,
    )
    return O
```

📌 **Code takeaways**:

1. **V1 parallel partition**: grid is `(batch, head)`, K/V outer, Q inner — each K/V tile loads from HBM once, but Q/O/m/l are read/written $T_c$ times
2. **Auxiliary M, L**: stats $m_i, l_i$ for Q must be shared across outer iters, so persist `[B, H, N]` tensors in HBM
3. **K/V OOB mask** (`tl.where(kv_mask, s, -inf)`) is critical for correctness; missing it pollutes the softmax denominator with fake zeros
4. **Initial `m, l`** are `-inf` and `0`; with `alpha = exp(-inf - m_new) = 0`, the first iter naturally becomes a normal accumulate
5. **V1 limit**: `(batch, head)` is usually far below SM count (A100 has 108 SMs; batch×head in inference is often only tens), so SM utilization is low; V2 puts Q on the grid to fix that

## 📝 Summary

FlashAttention V1 rethinks Attention through **IO-Awareness** and proposes Tiling + Online Softmax:

1. **No full $N \times N$ intermediate matrix** — tiled streaming avoids $O(N^2)$ memory
2. **Online Softmax stays exact** — maintain and correct local stats for results equivalent to standard Softmax
3. **Recompute instead of store** — backward recomputes $S$ and $P$, trading a little compute for large memory savings
4. **Asymptotically optimal IO** — HBM traffic drops from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N^2 d^2 / M)$
